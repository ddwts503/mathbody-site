Institute for Mathematical Body Science

数理身体科学研究所

We study the living body as a pressure system. Our research uses mathematical physics to explain how internal tissue pressure drives movement, fluid exchange and circulation, and to re-examine assumptions in physiology, medicine and sports science.

About the Institute

The Institute for Mathematical Body Science is a research division of the International Wellness Association, a general incorporated association registered in Japan. The Institute carries out theoretical research and publishes its results as open preprints and peer-reviewed articles.

Research Themes

Tissue Internal Pressure (TIP) theory

A framework treating the body as skeleton, muscle and pressure. Fascial compartments act as pressure vessels whose internal pressure contributes to force and joint motion, beyond what tendon elasticity alone can supply.

Interstitial fluid exchange

How cyclic compartment pressure during muscle contraction and relaxation repeatedly re-creates transient absorption at the capillary wall — a dynamic extension of the revised Starling principle.

Lymphatic mechanics

Mechanisms of initial lymphatic inflow driven by fascial shear and anchoring filaments, and time-dependent pressure gradients across lymphatic–venous anastomoses.

Musculoskeletal biomechanics

Boundary conditions in closed kinetic chains, the role of the hamstrings in hip and knee extension, and energy accounting in vertical jumping.

Bone formation

Bone formation as a pressure-driven fluid exchange phenomenon linking fascial tension, compartment pressure fluctuation and periosteal shear stress.

Bio-inspired actuators

Self-pressurizing hydrostatic actuators based on the multi-layer spiral architecture of deep fascia.

Researcher

Seiji Sato, D.D.S. — Director

Dentist and independent researcher. Part-time Lecturer, University of Human Arts and Sciences (Saitama, Japan). His theoretical work draws on three foundations: dentistry, clinical lymphatic care (developer of the Sato-style Lymphatic Care method), and mathematical physics. His long-term aim is to explain medicine, physiology and sports science through mathematical frameworks.
